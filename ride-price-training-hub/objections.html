Playbook · Section 5

Objection Handling

Clarify/Isolate → Acknowledge → Respond → Solution. Every “no” is an invitation to ask a question — discuss the concern before you sell anything.

🔎

No objections match that search.

1 · Set the stage early

Discovery, wants and needs, and rapport are imperative — what you uncover about driving habits shapes the base payment and menu setup.

2 · Relate it to them

Benefits should be specific, not general. Tie everything down with “Y.E.S.” statements: “You earlier said…”

3 · Know your numbers

Stay current on repair, maintenance, and cosmetic costs. Ask service for real repair orders and price changes over 3–5 years. Be an expert.

At the desk

Base payment objections

The play
“What is a comfortable payment? Great, and up to?”

Enter the payment amount into Ride Price — cash down adjusts automatically. Discuss the corrected down payment and explore the client's maximum.

Then close the loop
“Keep in mind this is a ballpark number. Next we will fill out a credit application, submit it to the lender and go to work for you to get the rate and term you deserve.”
The play
“What rate were you expecting? Great, from which lender did you see this rate? Excellent. I haven't earned the right to pull your credit yet. What we can do next is submit to the lender that you prefer and get the rate you deserve. After that, we can research other lenders to ensure you get a competitive rate.”
The play
“What term were you hoping for? Great! Do you have a preferred lender you work with? Excellent. I haven't earned the right to pull credit yet. What we can do next is submit to the lender that you prefer and we will get the term you qualify for. Sound fair?”

Have documents reflecting current market value and any reconditioning needed. The more transparent you are, the better. With an interactive trade evaluation, get your manager involved when any questions arise.

Discuss the benefits of the accessories they chose based on your discovery session. Confirm the benefits, build value, and reference the payment.

The play
“The accessories you've chosen will personalize and protect your vehicle with minimal impact on your payment. This is just a ballpark. What's a comfortable monthly payment for you? And up to?”

Then close on payment.

In the menu

Menu objections

Not an objection — it indicates skepticism.

The play
“No, you do not ‘have to,’ but I'm surprised you wouldn't want to. Why do you say that?”

Their response will reveal the specific objection, if any.

These products are ideal for people on a budget — if a higher payment breaks the budget, an expensive out-of-pocket repair will hurt far worse.

The play
“If that agreed payment is your budget, let me show you how protected you can be for that.”
  • Show the budget option with extended term and lower payment; use custom if necessary.
  • Do the math: a $4,000 touch screen ÷ 72 months is $55/month. “One repair like that means your $400 payment was really $455 — and that's just ONE repair.”

Always ask “Why?” — identify the reason with open-ended questions, then address it. Compare what's different now vs. their last vehicle:

  • Far more advanced technology and computers — collision mitigation, blind spot detection. “Even your mirrors are computerized now.”
  • Price inflation in parts and labor
  • Fragility of modern paint vs. extreme environmental factors
  • Dent repair is usually outsourced now
  • Identify whether the client consistently addresses cosmetic issues (check the trade). Were they meticulous about upkeep? Show how much they spent — and would save — with tire & wheel, dent, and paint coverage.
  • If they weren't, explain the resale value difference if they had been.
  • Explain how much more expensive new vehicles are to “self-insure” — with specific examples from your service department.

Products like VSC and GAP can be canceled at any time for a prorated amount: “Keep it while you own the vehicle, cancel it when you don't.”

The play
“Could you see yourself keeping this one longer?”
  • If their trade cycle matches factory coverage perfectly, discuss the benefits of a lease.
  • If trade cycle is under 5 years, focus on resale value: a well-maintained vehicle with accurate service history is worth more; dent/paint/T&W coverage lessens reconditioning deductions; big repairs get fixed before trade or come out of trade value. Either way, the client pays for it.
Product deep dive

Vehicle Service Contract objections

  • Reliability refers to the powertrain — what makes the vehicle mobile — not the longevity of electronics.
  • “A vehicle service department can't survive on just oil changes… I see the worst case scenario every day in my service drive.”
  • Apple/Samsung example: “Nothing is perfect — no brand can claim their product lasts forever without issues.”
  • Ask about the plan they had — powertrain, stated, or exclusionary coverage? They may not know what they bought. Be the expert and educate them again.
  • Explain why your VSC is different; the last dealer may have done them a disservice. Careful if they bought the last one from your dealer!
  • Ask about the specific issues they experienced — if covered under what you're offering, show it on the brochure.
The play
“I understand not wanting to have to pay for a repair, especially if I thought it was covered. That is exactly what this VSC will protect you from.”
  • VSC is priced on risk — by the end of factory coverage, the price goes up and the available time and miles go down.
  • Compare today's price and term against a VSC on a 3-year-old vehicle at today's pricing (then add inflation). It's far less desirable.
  • Typically you can't finance a VSC on its own — explain the benefit of deferring the cost inside their payment.
  • Added later, the client pays for a vehicle inspection and must remedy existing issues before coverage starts.

You should have known this already — Preferred and Standard terms should both coincide with the client's driving habits.

The play
“Tailor the coverage offered to match your specific driving habits.”

Some plans are long term/low miles, others short term/high miles — and everything in between.

  • “So did you like not having to pay for repairs?”
  • What was their last vehicle — was it as well-equipped as this one?
  • “Would you rather have it and not need it, or need it and not have it?”
Product deep dive

Pre-Paid Maintenance objections

  • Is your client a trained technician, or do they simply know how to change oil?
  • Can they identify and diagnose issues while they do it? Multi-point inspections are part of routine maintenance.
  • Do they use the recommended oil and OEM filters?
  • Do they keep records of the work they perform themselves? (Resale value!)
  • Show today's cost savings — everything out of pocket vs. the plan price, plus inflation: “How much was an oil change 3 years ago?”
  • Explain deferring the cost over the loan term vs. an out-of-pocket expense every service interval.
  • Complimentary drop-off and pick-up? Just make an appointment and that's it — convenience.
A client says “I'm all set, thanks.” — don't build more value yet.
Find out why they think they're all set. Discuss the concern first. Facts without questions come across as pushy.

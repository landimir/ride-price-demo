Leasing 101

Leasing may not be right for everyone — but as a qualified professional consultant, you help every client find the best way for them to take their next car home.

Why leasing matters

Benefits of leasing

For the consumer
  1. Term of ownership flexibility — new car, shorter contract, under warranty, newest technology and safety features, lower cost of ownership; family needs and the economy may change
  2. GAP included on most leases
  3. Possibility for lower payment
  4. One of the fastest ways to eliminate negative equity
  5. The money factor may beat the equivalent APR on incentivized leases (*2400 rule)
  6. The manufacturer owns the car and holds the liability — after an insured accident repair, devaluation hits the owner, not the consumer
For the dealer
  1. More cars sold, new and used — the new sold car, the turn/trade-in, and the next sold car. “Each leased car is a 3-car deal”
  2. Lease returns are great CPO units
  3. Potential for increased sold units — helps allocation long-term
  4. Faster turn time → more repeat and referral clients with a great selection of newer used cars
Apply it in Ride Price: know every field and how to explain it — tax toggle, MSRP vs. sell price, miles/year, residual (how it's determined and what it means), term, due at signing, upfront cash vs. cap cost reduction, money factor, acquisition fee, and payment. Learn your craft; teach and educate the client.
Speak the language

Basic terminology

Capitalized CostTotal agreed-upon cost of the vehicle.≈ amount financed
Cap Cost ReductionAny cash down payment, trade-in credits, or rebate amounts that reduce the amount being financed.≈ down payment
Adjusted Cap CostCapitalized cost minus cap cost reduction.
Cap Cost Reduction TaxSales tax due on cash paid at lease inception toward the leased vehicle.
Residual ValueThe estimated value of the vehicle at the scheduled end of the lease term — based on a percentage of MSRP.
Rent ChargeCharged in addition to depreciation and amortized amounts — like a finance charge, derived via the money factor.(Net Cap Cost + Residual) × Money Factor
Money FactorThe lease's financing rate.Money factor × 2400 ≈ APR
Acquisition FeeFee charged by the leasing company to arrange the lease. GAP waiver is part of it — but unlike a finance GAP policy, it won't cover the insurance deductible.
Disposition FeeThe turn-in fee to the manufacturer.
Base PaymentMonthly depreciation + monthly lease charge.
Monthly PaymentBase payment + monthly taxes based on the client's residence (some states collect up front).
Due at Signing vs. Cap Cost Reduction$2,500 due at signing vs. $2,500 minus the first month's payment going to cap cost reduction — know the difference for your state.
The exit strategy

Lease end options

Understanding the lease-end options makes clients comfortable with something new to them.

↩️ Turn In

Give the car back to the manufacturer. Best when the car is worth less than the residual or payoff — the client is protected against negative equity.

“You get to pay for less of the car's value than you used!”

🔄 Trade In

Sell the car to a dealer while buying a new one. Best when the car is worth more than the residual or payoff — the client keeps the positive equity.

“You can apply your equity like a down payment on a new car!”

🏁 Buy Out

Keep the car — finance or pay cash for the remaining balance. Enjoy the lower lease payment first, then finance only the residual value.

“Who better to buy a used car from than yourself?!”
“You get the car you want, at the payment you can afford, and at the end of your lease, you have options!”

🧠 Scenario drill

Your client's car was hit by hail. Even after insurance pays to fix it, the car is worth less because of the vehicle history. Which lease-end option is right for them — and why do they benefit?

Hint: the car is now worth less than its residual… who absorbs that loss on a turn-in?

Concerns to master

Common leasing objections

Know your manufacturer's excess wear and tear limit — which doesn't include tires. We also offer protection packages and products to minimize the client's out-of-pocket costs, giving them a worry-free ownership experience.

Know your manufacturer's excess mileage fees and the prepaid mileage fee. You can set up a higher-mileage lease and/or pre-purchase additional miles. Yes, the payment is higher — but they're far more likely to break even at lease end. Set up the lease properly: expectations for the miles and the money. They can also purchase the car at lease end plus taxes and fees.

Leasing is one of the best ways to eliminate negative equity! With negative equity, the average driver in a 72-month loan may not break even until months 48–60. Roll it into a properly set-up lease (correct miles/year), stay within wear/tear and mileage guidelines, and it's eliminated at the end of the term — back to $0 in 3 years instead of 5.

Use the discovery session to find their true buying pattern. Many clients believe they'll pay the car off and enjoy no payments — but their real history shows they never keep a car more than a few years. In that case, leasing gives them a newer car, a shorter term, and a lower payment.

The lease contract can look confusing. Cover the most important items and make sure they're clear on the contract, the insurance requirements, and GAP. Give them time to review it if they have specific questions.

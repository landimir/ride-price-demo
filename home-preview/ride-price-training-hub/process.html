Playbook · Section 1

The Process

From the first keystroke to the service walk — how a deal moves through Ride Price, and what a great client experience looks like at every step.

Step 1

Client Information Entry

Everything starts in Find a Customer. Ride Price checks client information against the CRM and all prior entries.

⚠ Use Google Chrome as your browser during your Ride Price experience — other browsers are not supported.
  1. Search first. Look the client up in Ride Price before anything else. If found, click edit and confirm their information is correct.
  2. Create second. For a new client, use the Create Customer icon at the top right — only after completing a search.
  3. Required information: first and last name, either email or phone number, address, and zip code.
Step 2

Discovery Session

“Describe a week in the life of your vehicle.” Trips · Family · Pets · Activities · Commute · Drive.

How it works

Build rapport and have an organic conversation. Upon fact finding and confirmation, enter the information into Ride Price — it pushes straight into the CRM. Click Next after each question, and Pick Vehicle after the final one.

What to uncover

  • Miles per year & average trade cycle
  • Where they park (inside/outside)
  • Plans for servicing and maintaining the vehicle
  • Outdoor activities, parking lots, highway driving, car washes
  • Pets? Children?

These Primary Buying Motivations drive your base payment and menu presentation later.

Step 3

Vehicle Selection

Filter between new, used, and certified pre-owned — colors, trims, mileage, and price — then confirm the vehicle against their buying motivations.

Confirm the fit

Upon choosing a vehicle, confirm it has the features your client is asking for. The vehicle page shows the description, options, and tech specs.

The “Your Journey” menu

  • Test Drive — opens the test drive agreement
  • Quote — quick quote only; not for use during a client visit
  • Save Quote — save in Ride Price and email the structure to your client
  • Trade Appraisal — trigger screen for an active trade
  • Calculate Payment — skips test drive, straight to base payment
Best practice: test drive the vehicle before the trade evaluation or showing payments.
Step 4

Test Drive Agreement

Verify client info against their driver license, confirm the current address, and add their auto insurance company to the agreement.

Word track — introducing e-signatures
“You'll find many of the things we do here are different from traditional dealerships; and one of the ways we're different is by using electronic signatures. These are legally binding signatures just like ink signatures, and you're authorizing the use of your electronic signature.”
  1. Choose Printed documents, then offer the client the option to Type or Draw their signature. Select the acknowledgement box and click Continue.
  2. Enter the driver license #, additional drivers (if applicable), issuing state, expiration date, and the mileage you'll be driving.
  3. Explain the liabilities in the agreement. Client signs and accepts terms; the client advisor also types or draws a signature. Click Next.
  4. Going on an extended solo drive? Print the agreement and use Go to Test Drive List.
  5. After the drive, click End, enter the current miles, and click Complete Test Drive.
Step 5

Trade Evaluation

Introduce your friend, the trade value expert, giving an interactive experience to get them top dollar for their trade.

The introduction

Click Trade Evaluation to preview the overview with your client — and give a proper introduction to your team lead. No trade? Go straight to Calculate Payment.

What the client hears

  • We obtain your vehicle's VIN and actual mileage
  • Our evaluator accesses book values, auction values, and true market values of vehicles just like yours
  • We invite you on an interactive walk-around and short drive — who knows your car better than you?
Transparency wins trades: have documents reflecting current market value and any reconditioning needed. Get your manager involved when questions arise.
Step 8 · After Base Payment

Lending Lane — the credit application

Once your client signs base payment, fill out the digital credit application together. All of it happens inside Ride Price.

  1. Open the deal from the Deals list → Actions → Credit Application.
  2. Co-buyer? Click Joint at the top before entering information (per Regulation B).
  3. Complete every asterisk field. Ensure proper time amounts for residency and employment per your dealership requirements.
  4. Click I Agree / Submit — your processing department submits to lenders for approval.
  5. Gather the deal documents: driver's license, trade title, vehicle registration, and an updated insurance card if your state requires one.
While processing works on the bank approval, begin the Manufacturer Warranty Overview and Service Walk — and print your Cover Sheet to confirm you have every item for the deal.
Step 9

Manufacturer Warranty Overview & Service Walk

Discuss the remaining factory coverage and how the client's driving habits impact it. Do not sell or plant seeds for any products at this time — this builds future value for the menu.

  • In-service date — today's date for new and used vehicles; the original sale date for a Certified Pre-Owned.
  • Powertrain warranty — covers the internally lubricated parts of the engine and transmission (only what oil and transmission fluid touch). Electronics, computers, and sensors fall under the comprehensive warranty.
  • Real coverage math — a 3-year / 36,000-mile comprehensive warranty expires in 2 years and 4 months at 15,000 miles a year; the 5-year / 60,000-mile powertrain in 4 years.

The exclusions

🧰 Maintenance

The owner maintains the vehicle per the owner's manual.

🚨 Accident / Total Loss

The owner must carry insurance coverage on the vehicle.

✨ Appearance

Paint clarity & clear coat, shine, gloss, damaged or lost key remotes, and interior stains aren't covered.

🛞 Road Hazard

Bent or damaged wheels, damaged tires, dents, dings, chipped or broken glass aren't covered.

⏳ Wear & Tear

Worn tires, brake pads, belts, hoses, interior leather or fabric aren't covered by the manufacturer.

🤝 Service Walk

After the M.W.O., show clients the service department, introduce an advisor, and book their first appointment — it raises retention and sets up finance products.

The Support System

Who does what

Team Lead & Processing
  • The Team Lead signs off on the deal and delivers it to the Processing Team
  • Processing handles all finance operations: pulling credit, submitting to lenders, getting deals bought, CIT, and funding
  • Team Leads build the finance menu after approval and own the DMS push
General Finance Duties
  • Financial: protect the dealer · sell product · cash contracts · deliver vehicle · legal compliance
  • Product knowledge: coverage, terms, exclusions, claims process, client applicability
  • Introduce products through the sales process; use the trade-in to set up product sales

Playbook · Section 2

Base Payment

Present the base payment while earning credibility through transparency. Get in the financial ballpark, discover concerns, and set up the menu for success.

Before the numbers

The Base Payment Huddle

Touch the desk — the post-demo team lead huddle. The team lead and client advisor will “game plan” the initial pencil.

Huddle questions

  • What was the answer to the trial close?
  • How are they paying for the car?
  • Ask for trade evaluation (if necessary)
  • Do we have the car in stock today?
  • Is there anything else?

Payment options discovery

  • Cash — check or cashier's check? “Where will we be sending the title?”
  • Lease — frequent trader? low-mileage driver? “Have you ever considered leasing?”
  • Finance — incentivized rate, or is a rebate better? “Can we arrange your financing on your behalf?”
Setting up the deal

T.A.C.C. — Trade, Accessories, Cash down, Credit

Accessories

  • Present accessories that match their primary buying motivations
  • Let them design and personalize their vehicle the way they dream it — before payments
  • Enhanced ownership: protection, excitement, individuality — backed by brand and dealer

Cash down

Ask how much they were planning on putting down. Get a range or an “up to” number.

Credit

  • Asking about credit means you never assume creditworthiness
  • Don't know their score? Have them rate their credit 1–5
  • Know it? Ask the source — general pulls run 20–40 points higher than vehicle-specific pulls. Conservative or optimistic?
The presentation

Presenting the base payment

You have the flexibility to show finance, lease, and cash. Establish a baseline with shorter terms and an average rate — it sets up the finance menu. Discuss incentives and rebates based on eligibility, and discover any concerns.

Presentation order

Finance — 7 steps
  1. Disclose MSRP, selling price, included options, and your price “including the accessories you chose of X, Y & Z”
  2. “Including your trade” (or “including paying off your trade”)
  3. Congratulate on rebates (if applicable)
  4. “Including taxes and fees…”
  5. “At a standard term, based on the credit you provided…”
  6. Confirm down payment
  7. “Your monthly payment is $…”
Lease — 8 steps
  1. Confirm annual mileage
  2. Confirm due at signing
  3. Disclose MSRP, selling price, included options, and your price with chosen accessories
  4. Disclose residual value — what they can buy the vehicle for at lease end, plus taxes and fees
  5. “Including your trade” (or “including paying off your trade”)
  6. Congratulate on rebates
  7. “Including taxes and fees…”
  8. “At a standard term, based on the credit you provided, your monthly payment is $…”

Full word tracks

Finance base payment presentation
“For your 2022 Hyundai Santa Fe, the MSRP is $42,910. Your selling price is $41,431. With the included options and the accessories you chose of all season mats and window tint, your price is $43,121. With us paying off your trade and congratulations, you qualified for the $500 rebate, including your taxes and fees, at a standard 60 month term, based on the credit score you provided, you are still putting down $1,000 correct? Your payment is $546.66.”
Lease base payment presentation
“For your 2022 Hyundai Santa Fe, these lease terms are based on 12,000 miles per year, is that correct? And with your $1,000 due at signing, correct? Excellent. The MSRP is $42,910. Your selling price is $41,431. With the included options and the accessories you chose of all season mats and window tint, your price is $43,121. If you wanted to purchase your vehicle at the end of the lease, you could do so for $27,462.40 PLUS TAXES & FEES. With us paying off your trade, and congratulations, you qualified for the $500 rebate, including your taxes and fees, at a standard 36 month term, based on the credit you provided, your payment is $423.29.”
🤫 Stop talking! Wait for your client to respond. If in agreement, click  CONTINUE.

Cash & one-pay lease

Cash

The cash flow mirrors a finance deal structure — minus payment, term, and rate. Present the Total Due and walk each tax and fee.

One-Pay Lease

Follow the same flow to present the one-pay option (a single due-at-signing amount). If agreed, click Continue, have the client sign electronically, and click Save.

Tools

Compare Payments

Under the More… dropdown at the top right, Compare Payments shows payment options specific to your deal — including trade-in value and the accessories you've sold — side by side (e.g., finance $692.41 vs. lease $408.44).

Rebates and cash down will not carry over when changing deal types. Click Save to select your deal type.
Sealing the ballpark

Commitment to Buy

Set the proper expectations: explain what the next steps are and how long they should take.

Word track — transitioning forward
“Next, we can fill out our digital credit application. I'm going to get all your information to our processing department. Once I finish that up, we'll go over your final numbers and the rest of your options.”
Remember: your client is not buying the vehicle at this point. They are agreeing to a ballpark structure from base payment.
  • Fill out the digital credit application in Lending Lane
  • Gather driver's license, trade title, vehicle registration, and any documents the deal requires
  • Use the Ride Price Cover Sheet checklist — this transition can be efficient or add time to the whole process
Paper trail

Terms of Agreement & deal controls

The signed base payment agreement gets printed and placed in the deal folder. From the Deals list you control everything that follows.

Edit Deal

Used by the team lead to begin building the finance menu — completed after the approval is received.

Deals List

Actions: edit menu, change deal, assign a lender, credit application.
Forms: print or preview the cover sheet, base payment agreement, applied rebates, customer forms, and contracts.

Redesk Payment

Reopens the base payment screen to adjust details and structure — this voids your previously signed base payment.
